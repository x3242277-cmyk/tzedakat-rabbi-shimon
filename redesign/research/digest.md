# Research digest (collected earlier from web research; ideas to borrow, never copy a brand)

## research:premium
Borrow the 'quiet luxury' register of private banking and premium card products: a warm near-black canvas built from a few lightness steps, ivory text, and a single pale champagne gold spent sparingly on the things that represent value (the total, progress, the primary action). Depth comes from hairline borders, inner highlights, a faint ambient glow and grain, with one glass panel and one gilded card per screen. Typography does the storytelling: a Hebrew-capable serif display face (Frank Ruhl Libre is an open-licence candidate) for hero numerals and headings, paired with a clean Hebrew sans (such as Heebo) with tabular figures for all data. The 'cool' moments are concentrated in a few signature objects: the giant counting total, the member card that tilts and catches light, the watch-bezel ring, the tier ladder and the milestone tracker, plus a restrained foil-sweep when a target is hit. The palette hex values and opacity figures given here are my own starting suggestions to tune, not established standards. Research caveat: web search worked, but page fetching was blocked in this session (figr.design and medium.com are outside the allowed network domains and would have to be allowed in the session's network settings), so findings rest on search-result summaries, which were mostly agency, template and practitioner pages rather than primary bank or insurer sites, combined with my own knowledge of these product categories. No specific bank's live site was inspected directly.

- **Hero balance numeral** (Wealth-management client portals and advisor dashboards (highlighted net-worth figure with trend arrow and drill-down)): One oversized figure owns the top of the page, the way a wealth portal leads with net worth. Serif display numeral at roughly clamp(2.5rem, 6vw, 4rem) or larger, currency sign at about 55% size and lower contrast, a small delta chip beside it (arrow plus change since last period), and a faint sparkline or area chart behind or beside it. Counts up once on load in 1 to 1.5 seconds with ease-out. The tile is clickable and drills into the breakdown. → here: Top tile: total collected this year in shekels as the single biggest object on screen, with the yearly target and percent reached as a quiet second line, a chip for 'this week +X', and a 12-week sparkline. Wrap the number in an LTR-isolated span so the shekel sign, digits and percent do not reorder inside the RTL layout. Same component, smaller, heads each student page.
- **Obsidian layered surfaces with hairlines** (Dark-first fintech and business-banking dashboards; quiet-luxury style references): No pure black and no pure white. A warm near-black base (start around #0B0C0F), with panels lifted in two or three small lightness steps (#121419, #191C22, #21252D). Structure comes from 1px low-alpha borders (white at about 8%) and an inset 1px top highlight, not from drop shadows, which barely read on dark. Text is warm ivory (#ECE8DF) with secondary and tertiary levels made by opacity (about 64% and 40%). → here: The whole app shell: sidebar, cards, drawers and modals each sit on the next surface step. This alone removes the spreadsheet feel because cells and gridlines are replaced by floating panels with depth. Keep one radius (for example 16px cards, 10px controls) everywhere.
- **Rationed champagne gold** (Black-and-gold premium card and private-banking branding; gold UI colour guides): Gold is an accent budget, under roughly 10% of pixels. Use a pale, slightly desaturated champagne (#D8C08A) rather than yellow, with a deep bronze (#8C6E3A) and a pale highlight (#F1E3BD) to build a matte three- or four-stop gradient at about 135 degrees. Gold appears only on: the hero numeral or its underline, progress fills, the active nav item, the primary button, and tier marks. Buttons filled with gold carry dark ink text, not white. Champagne on the near-black base gives roughly 10:1 contrast, so it is safe for large numerals. → here: Collected amounts, target-progress fills and the one primary action per screen (for example 'record donation') are gold; everything else is ivory and grey. Status colours stay muted and separate (soft emerald for ahead of pace, muted terracotta for behind) so gold always means 'value', never 'warning'.
- **Gilded-edge feature card** (Premium dark SaaS and fintech landing pages and dashboards (gradient border, inner highlight, spotlight hover)): A single card per screen gets a gradient hairline border: a masked pseudo-element whose 1px stroke runs from champagne at one corner to transparent at the opposite one, plus an inset top highlight and a soft radial spotlight that follows the pointer on hover. All other cards keep plain hairlines, which is what makes this one feel special. → here: Use it on the hero total tile on the home dashboard, and on the currently selected student or class card in lists. In RTL, start the gold at the top-right corner so the light appears to come from the reading-start side.
- **Ambient glow, grain, and one glass panel** (Dark fintech dashboards and 2026 glass-bento concepts; luxury dashboard style references): Behind the content sit two very large, heavily blurred radial gradients (a deep navy or bottle green, and a warm gold at about 6 to 10% opacity), with a faint SVG noise overlay (start around 4 to 8%) so the gradients do not band and the surface feels like material. Exactly one element uses real glass (translucent fill plus backdrop blur), and it sits over the glow, because glass over a flat background looks like plain grey. → here: Static glow behind the hero area of the home page and the login screen. Make the sticky top bar or the floating 'quick add donation' panel the single glass element. Keep the data area below on solid surfaces for legibility.
- **Member card** (Premium card and neobank apps (interactive 3D card viewers, metal-card landing pages); digital insurance ID cards in insurtech apps): A landscape card with physical-card proportions and a matte metal finish: fine engraved line pattern (guilloche-style) at very low contrast, a thin lighter rim to suggest edge thickness, name and a monospaced member number, a small tier mark. On desktop it tilts a few degrees toward the pointer with a specular light band that sweeps across; on touch devices and under reduced-motion it is static. → here: Every student gets a card as the header of their page: name, class or group, student code, year, current tier, and percent of yearly target. In the roster, a 'wallet stack' view shows cards overlapping, as an alternative to the table. The card's metal tone follows the tier. Design an original pattern and mark; do not mimic any real issuer's card.
- **Tier ladder with distance to next level** (Airline, bank and retail loyalty apps (tier-progress components, tier-journey visuals)): A horizontal journey track showing all levels at once (for example four metals), the member's current position marked, and one plain sentence stating the exact distance to the next level. Thresholds are stable numbers that never move day to day. Shown on the first screen, not buried in a sub-tab. → here: Map percent of yearly target to tiers (for example 25, 50, 75, 100%). On the student page: the track plus 'X shekels to the next level'. On the admin dashboard: a compact distribution strip showing how many students sit in each tier. Name tiers positively and keep the bottom tier neutral so nobody is shamed.
- **Milestone tracker with next-action panel** (Insurance claim trackers and policyholder portals (timeline plus progress bar; next-action panel)): The claim-status pattern: a stepper where completed nodes are filled and timestamped, the current node has a soft pulsing ring, future nodes are outlined. Major milestones are visually heavier than minor events so the timeline is not a flat log. Beside it sits a separate small panel answering only 'what do I need to do now'. → here: Yearly campaign track for each student and for the whole organisation: start, 25%, 50%, 75%, target reached, each with the date it was hit. The next-action panel says for example 'collect X per week to reach the target by the end of the year' or 'two donors promised and not yet recorded'. Flows right to left in RTL.
- **Pace-versus-target projection chart** (Goal-based investing and savings apps (on-track versus off-track progress); wealth client portals (goal-centric timelines)): A cumulative line with a soft gradient area fill underneath, a thin dashed line for the ideal pace to the goal, and a dotted forecast continuing from today to the end date. A status pill above reads 'on track' or 'behind pace'. Minimal axes, no gridline cage, one crosshair tooltip with tabular numerals. → here: Replaces plain bar charts. Organisation level: cumulative collected through the year against the straight-line pace to the yearly target, with a forecast of where the year will end. Student level: the same chart, smaller. Gold for actual, grey dashed for pace, lighter dotted for forecast.
- **Watch-bezel goal ring** (Savings-goal and wealth apps; premium dark fintech dashboards): A thick circular arc with round caps and a gold gradient stroke on a dark track, fine tick marks around the outside like a watch bezel, and a large serif percentage in the centre with a one-line caption underneath. The arc draws in once on load. Overachievement is shown by a second, thinner lap rather than clipping at 100%. → here: Yearly target completion for the organisation, plus a row of small rings, one per class or group, for instant comparison. Keep the ring clockwise even in RTL (clock direction is not mirrored), while linear bars fill from the right.
- **Bento layout with staggered reveal** (2026 fintech dashboard concepts and product marketing pages): A grid of mixed-size tiles where size encodes importance: one large hero tile, two medium, several small. Tiles fade and rise in sequence (about 40 to 60 ms apart) on first load only. Dense working screens fall back to a normal grid. → here: Home dashboard: large hero total tile, goal ring tile, top performers tile, recent donations feed, milestone track, days-remaining tile. Roster and data-entry screens stay as straightforward lists so the app remains fast to use.
- **Statement-grade ledger table** (Private-banking and brokerage portals (holdings and transaction statements); fintech data-visualisation guidelines): The anti-Excel table: no vertical lines, generous rows (about 56px), hairline row dividers only, avatar or initials plus name in the lead column, amounts in sans with tabular figures aligned to one edge, an inline micro progress bar and a status pill per row, a soft surface lift on hover, quiet sticky header labels. Clicking a row opens a side drawer instead of navigating away. → here: The student roster and the donations list. Columns: student, class, collected, target, micro progress bar, tier mark, last activity. Sorting and filters live in a slim toolbar of pill chips above the table. Serif numerals are reserved for hero figures; table numbers stay sans with tabular-nums.
- **Top performers podium** (Wealth apps (top holdings and movers lists); loyalty dashboards): Instead of a ranked bar chart, the top three are larger cards in restrained metal tones (champagne, cool silver, muted bronze) with rank numeral in serif, and the rest follow as a compact ranked list with small up or down movement arrows since last period. → here: Top students and top classes this month. Offer a toggle between 'highest amount' and 'highest percent of own target' so smaller targets can also lead. Show movement arrows week over week to make it feel alive.
- **Big-number editorial band** (Annual-report microsites and insurer or asset-manager 'in numbers' sections; animated-counter guidance): A horizontal strip of three or four statistics set like an annual report: large serif numerals, a tiny caption under each, separated by vertical hairlines, lots of empty space. The unit carries the meaning, so captions are human ('families helped') rather than technical. Counters run only when scrolled into view and finish in one to two seconds. → here: Under the hero: number of donors, average per student, students who reached target, days left in the year. If the charity can translate money into outcomes, add one line such as 'equals N food baskets'. Reuse the band as the header of a shareable year-end summary.
- **Activity ledger and coordinator card** (Wealth-management and private-banking client portals; insurtech apps that surface charitable-giving impact inside the account): Private-bank portals pair a recent-activity list (icon, description, signed amount, relative time) with a small 'your advisor' card showing a face, a name and one contact action. New ledger entries slide in with a brief gold shimmer and then settle. → here: A live feed of recent donations: student, donor label, amount with a plus sign, time. The newest entry is highlighted for a few seconds. The coordinator card shows the staff member responsible for each group, with a message button, which makes the tool feel like a serviced account rather than a spreadsheet.
- **Motion grammar and foil-sweep celebration** (Digital-bank savings-goal features (motion marking progress and achievement); dark business-banking dashboards (motion used sparingly for state changes)): One consistent set of motions: numbers count up, bars and arcs draw from the reading-start side, tiles stagger in, hover adds a spotlight. Motion marks state changes only and never loops. Reaching a milestone triggers a single diagonal light sweep across the relevant card (like foil catching light) and a tier-mark swap, instead of confetti. Everything collapses to instant under prefers-reduced-motion. → here: When a student crosses 25, 50, 75 or 100% of the yearly target, their member card gets the sweep and upgrades its tier tone; the milestone node fills and gets its date. On data refresh, only changed numbers animate (a short roll from old to new value).

Avoid: Pure #000 backgrounds with pure #FFF text; use warm near-black and ivory instead, since the hard contrast is tiring and reads as cheap.; Saturated yellow or orange-leaning 'gold' (for example #FFD700), flat single-colour gold fills, and glossy chrome or bevelled metallic gradients. Keep gold pale, matte and multi-stop.; Gold on large surfaces, gold body text, or gold used for more than about a tenth of the screen. If everything is gold, nothing is premium.; Glow, gradient borders or blur on every component. One gilded card and one glass element per screen.; Glass panels over a flat background; without an ambient gradient behind them they look like plain grey boxes and cost performance for nothing.; Heavy drop shadows on dark surfaces; they do not read. Use lightness steps and hairlines.; Rainbow or holographic effects beyond the member card, neon accents, and more than two accent hues besides gold.; Counters longer than about two seconds, looping animations, and re-animating the whole page on every data refresh.; Pointer-tilt, shader or 3D effects on mobile and low-end devices, or without a reduced-motion fallback.; Serif numerals inside dense tables, or proportional figures in number columns. Tables need sans with tabular figures.; More than two type families. For Hebrew: avoid wide letter-spacing and faux small caps (Hebrew has no capitals); make labels quiet by size and opacity instead.; Unisolated numbers in RTL text; the currency sign, percent sign and minus can jump sides. Isolate numeric runs as LTR.; Tier thresholds or targets that move from day to day, and timelines where every event has equal visual weight.; Tier names or leaderboards that shame students at the bottom; show percent of own target as an alternative ranking.; Literal luxury cliches: coin and gold-bar imagery, crests, laurels, marble stock photos, ornate script fonts.; Copying any specific bank's or card issuer's card layout, logo placement, patterns or brand colours; borrow the mechanics only.; Grids, cell borders, zebra stripes and default chart-library colours, which are exactly what produced the 'Excel' impression.

## research:neobank
The shared language of the best neo-bank and business-banking dashboards is calm confidence rather than decoration: a near-neutral canvas (soft off-white, or deep graphite instead of pure black), one saturated accent used sparingly, and money set in large tabular numerals at a display weight so the figures themselves are the visual. Structure comes from flat rounded cards (about 12-16px radius, hairline border or a slight tonal lift, almost no shadow) and pill-shaped controls on a 4px spacing grid. Colour is semantic and disciplined: green for money in, amber for pending, red only for things that need attention, all slightly desaturated so they sit together. Gradients and richer colour are confined to a few 'objects' (account cards, the completed-goal state, the year-in-review story), which is what makes them feel special. Motion is reserved for three things: staggered entrance, numbers changing, and genuine celebration. For this project, pair that with a strong Hebrew typeface that has good numerals, keep amounts in isolated LTR spans, and mirror layout, carousels, drawers and progress fills for RTL. Research caveat: web search worked, but opening pages was blocked by the sandbox network allow-list (www.flatstudio.co, stories.fylehq.com and playbooks.com failed to resolve, so I stopped fetching); findings therefore rest on search-result summaries of the listed pages, which are mostly third-party teardowns and agency write-ups rather than the companies' own design documentation, supplemented by my own knowledge where marked.

- **One hero number that answers the first question** (Business-banking and spend-management web dashboards; described in third-party teardowns of balance-first banking homes. The masking toggle is from my own knowledge of consumer banking apps (search found no write-up).): The signed-in home opens on a single figure that is the largest text on the page (roughly 48-72px, display weight, tabular numerals), with a small muted label above it, a delta chip beside it (pill, tinted green/amber, arrow + amount + period), and a segmented time-range control (week / month / year) that re-scopes the whole hero. Everything else on the page is visibly subordinate. Business-banking homes lead with total balance, spend tools with total spend, payment tools with gross volume. An eye icon next to the figure masks all amounts on the page (dots or blur) for privacy. → here: Hero = total collected this year, in shekels, with 'out of [yearly target]' in muted text right under it and a chip such as '+4,250 this week'. Segmented control: week / month / year-to-date. For a student the hero is their own total; for staff it is the whole institution. Add the eye toggle so the screen can be projected in a hall without exposing sums. Keep digits and the currency sign in an isolated LTR span inside the RTL layout so the number never reorders.
- **Hero that flips between trend chart and breakdown table** (A startup business bank's homepage balance module (documented in its help centre: table icon next to the balance toggles breakdown vs balance graph).): A small icon pair at the corner of the hero toggles the same module between (a) a smooth area/line chart of the headline figure over time with a hover crosshair and floating tooltip, and (b) a compact table that decomposes the number into its parts. Same card, same height, cross-fade between the two; the choice is remembered. → here: Chart view: cumulative collection through the year with a dashed 'required pace' line running to the yearly target, so being above or below the line is readable in one glance. Table view: the total split into cash, credit card, standing orders, cheques and pledged-not-yet-received. Staff who think in tables still get their table, but it lives inside a designed object instead of being the whole page.
- **Settled vs pending split inside the headline** (Business-banking balance breakdowns (available / pending outgoing / treasury) and cross-border transfer dashboards that lead with pending transfers.): The headline separates money that is really there from money on its way: 'available' in full-strength colour, 'pending' in a lighter tint or diagonal hatch, shown both as two labelled sub-figures and as two segments of one bar. Some transfer products lead with the pending items because that is what the user is anxious about. → here: One wide progress bar toward the yearly target with three segments: deposited (solid accent), pledged/awaiting deposit (hatched tint of the accent), remaining (neutral track). Hovering a segment shows amount and count. This turns 'pledges' from a hidden spreadsheet column into a visible, motivating part of the picture. Fill direction runs right-to-left.
- **Account cards as physical objects: themed carousel or stack** (A large European neo-bank's 2023 app overhaul (all accounts on home, per-account themes and backgrounds, home widgets) and generic card-stack slider patterns.): Accounts are shown as rounded cards (16-20px radius) with their own gradient or background theme, a name, the amount, and a tiny identifier, arranged in a horizontally snapping carousel or a stack where the edges of the next cards peek out behind the active one. Tapping a card re-scopes the page to that account. Users can pick a theme per account so they are told apart by colour before reading. Premium tiers get a distinct darker or metallic gradient. → here: Each class / group / campaign becomes a card with its own gradient, name, collected amount and a thin progress line along the bottom edge; selecting one filters hero, feed and leaderboard. A student gets a personal 'collector card' styled like a bank card (name, group, personal target, percent) that sits at the top of their home. A special gradient unlocks at 100% of target, which gives a visible status object without any shaming of others. In RTL the carousel starts from the right and the peeking cards appear on the left.
- **Quick-action row, capped at four or five** (Neo-bank mobile home screens and a virtual-card platform's dashboard redesign; secondary design guides describing five-action home screens.): Directly under the hero: a single row of circular icon buttons (44-56px) with one-word labels beneath, the primary one filled with the accent and the rest tonal. Teardowns repeatedly note the discipline: around five actions on home, everything else one level down. Some apps let the user pin their own shortcuts as widgets. → here: Record donation (primary, filled), Add donor, Issue receipt, Share my collection link, Export. Nothing else on that row. Pressing a button gives a 0.96 scale press state and opens a side sheet rather than navigating away, so the dashboard stays in view behind it.
- **Exception-first 'needs attention' queue** (Spend-management dashboards organised around approve / match / close; third-party analyses recommending exception-first design.): Instead of charts for their own sake, the home has a work queue: a card titled with a count, listing only the items that need a human, each row with an avatar, one line of explanation, and an inline action button. What is on track is deliberately hidden. When the queue is empty it collapses into a calm one-line 'all clear' state with a check mark. Red is reserved for this module so it keeps its meaning. → here: Staff view: 'Needs attention (7)' listing students behind pace by more than a set margin, donations awaiting confirmation, cheques not yet deposited, collectors with no activity for 14 days. Inline actions: Confirm, Send reminder, Open student. Student view: 'Your next steps' with at most three items. Agree with the charity what counts as an exception before building it.
- **Insight chips and one-question insight cards** (Consumer finance apps' monthly-spend banners and weekly summaries; SaaS finance dashboards with optional insight cards; analytics tools where each card's title is the question it answers.): A horizontally scrolling strip of small cards or pills, each stating one finding as a full sentence in its title, with a tiny supporting visual (sparkline, mini bar, single big figure) and an optional drill-down. Typical content: weekly summary, comparison with the previous period, an unusual-activity flag, a forecast. Cards are dismissible, and some products let power users turn the strip off in favour of a full-width table. → here: Auto-generated Hebrew sentences: 'Group 3 passed 75% this week', 'At the current pace the yearly target is reached about six weeks early', 'Thursday is your strongest day', 'Three new donors this month'. Each chip has a coloured leading icon and opens a filtered view. Include the forecast chip always: a projected finish date is the single most 'financial-product' feeling element you can add.
- **Goal 'pots' with progress ring, pace and projected date** (UK neo-bank savings pots with goals (community feature threads and a UX article on money habits); goal-based savings design case studies.): Each goal is a tile with a name, an emoji-free icon or cover image, a circular progress ring (stroke 8-10px, rounded caps, percent in the centre), collected / target figures, and a secondary line with the projection ('on track for March'). Rings animate from zero on first paint. Goal-tracking features in neo-banks consistently combine: target amount, progress, contribution nudges, timeline projection and milestone rewards. A summary card above the grid shows total progress across all goals. → here: Every student is a pot: ring with percent, 'collected of target', a pace pill (ahead / on pace / behind, in green / neutral / amber, never red), and projected finish date. Staff see a grid of pots sortable by percent, pace or last activity; a student sees their own pot large plus the group pot. Tick marks on the ring at 25/50/75% show the next milestone and how much is missing to reach it.
- **Celebration reserved for real milestones** (Payment-success patterns in fintech apps and animation guidance for banking interfaces; analysis of a large neo-bank's restrained motion.): Motion is kept off ordinary data and spent on a few moments: on success a circle draws, a check mark strokes in, the amount confirms, and only then a brief particle burst fires (well under two seconds, a lighter version for small events). A soft glow or colour pulse accompanies a number going up. The principle stated in teardowns: clean data display, animation saved for celebratory moments. → here: Trigger only on: donation recorded (small: check draws, hero ticks up), a student crossing 25/50/75% (medium: ring pulse plus toast), and a student or group reaching 100% (full: burst, card switches to its 'completed' gradient, a banner in the feed everyone sees). A milestones strip under the hero shows achieved ones as filled medallions and the next one as an outline with the amount still missing.
- **Number animation and staggered entrance** (Animation-principle write-ups for fintech and banking UIs; portfolio case studies of finance dashboards (timings are starting points, not standards).): Monetary figures never just appear: they count up or roll digit by digit, with a short anticipation and a fast ease-out reveal (about 250ms for an update, up to roughly 600-800ms for the first load), using tabular numerals so width does not jitter. The page builds in order: headline figure first, then cards, then the activity list; charts draw their axes first and the line afterwards; bars grow from the baseline. All of it is disabled under reduced-motion settings. → here: On load the hero total counts up while the progress bar and rings fill in sync; cards fade and rise 8px with a 40-60ms stagger. When a donation is saved, the hero rolls only the changed digits and the affected student's ring advances, so the user sees cause and effect. Keep the numeral container LTR and fixed-width inside the RTL page.
- **Activity feed grouped by day with avatars and a detail sheet** (Neo-bank transaction lists; an open-source wallet's transaction-list component spec; a card network's list-view vs detail-view guidance; user feedback threads asking for day grouping.): The transaction list is a first-class home module, not hidden behind 'see all'. Rows are grouped under sticky day headers ('Today', 'Yesterday', then dates); each row has a 40px round avatar or logo, a bold name, a muted second line (category, method, time), the amount aligned to the far edge in tabular numerals with a sign and colour for incoming, and a small status pill for pending items. Tapping a row opens a sheet or side drawer that repeats the avatar and name and adds full details and actions. Search and filter chips sit above the list. → here: Live donations feed: student avatar (initials on a coloured disc, colour derived from the group), donor name, payment-method icon, amount in green with a plus sign, status pill (confirmed / pending / returned). Day headers carry the day's subtotal and count. Row click opens a drawer from the left edge (RTL) with receipt, note, edit and 'send thank-you'. New rows slide in at the top with a brief highlight so the screen feels alive.
- **Bento layout with density matched to role** (Spend-management and expense dashboards (admin vs team-member homes); analyses contrasting dense finance-team views with minimal founder views.): The home is a grid of rounded widgets of mixed sizes (one wide hero, a few medium cards, small stat tiles) rather than equal KPI boxes, collapsing to a single column on narrow screens. Products tune density to the audience: occasional users get a minimal glance view, professionals who live in the tool get denser tables and controls. One expense tool's personal home is exactly five widgets: spend, cards, incomplete items, requests/approvals, updates. → here: Student home (minimal): personal card + ring, next milestone, quick actions, own recent donations, place in group. Staff home (dense): hero with chart/table flip, needs-attention queue, group cards carousel, leaderboard, live feed, insights strip. Same components, two compositions, so the build stays small. Let staff reorder or hide widgets later; it is a cheap 'premium' feature.
- **Sidebar navigation plus a command palette** (Business SaaS and finance tools with Cmd+K palettes; guidance from an email client's write-up on building command palettes. (Search did not confirm any specific bank's palette, so treat as a general pro-tool pattern.)): Web: a slim persistent sidebar with icon + label items and the organisation switcher at the top; a search field in the top bar that opens a centred command palette (Ctrl/Cmd+K) listing pages, people and actions in grouped results, fully keyboard-navigable, Escape to close. The palette is a shortcut layered over clear navigation, not a substitute. Mobile: bottom tab bar with four or five items and a raised primary action in the centre. → here: Sidebar on the right (RTL): Home, Students, Donors, Donations, Reports, Settings. Ctrl+K opens a Hebrew palette: type a student or donor name to jump to them, or an action such as 'record donation' or 'export month'. On phones: bottom bar with a prominent central 'record donation' button. Staff who enter dozens of donations a day will feel the speed immediately.
- **Year-in-review story with a shareable card** (Year-end recaps from UK, Nordic, Australian and Georgian digital banks and a buy-now-pay-later app.): A full-screen, tap-to-advance sequence of bold single-stat cards (one number and one sentence per screen, large type, saturated backgrounds, simple motion), opened from a banner on home. Variants seen: a tone choice at the start, an opening quiz that asks the user to guess before revealing, a persona or character assigned at the end, and a long-press or button to save a ready-made share image. One bank reported very high open and share rates for this format. → here: End-of-year (and mid-year) recap per student and per group: total collected, number of donors, best week, biggest jump, percent of target, a title such as 'Marathon runner' or 'Fast starter', ending in a card image sized for WhatsApp. For staff: an institution-wide version to show at the closing event. This is the 'gimmick' the client is asking for, and it reuses data you already have.
- **Friendly leaderboard tied to real actions** (Gamification analyses of fintech and digital-banking apps (mostly agency and trade write-ups).): A podium for the top three (larger avatars, medal accents) above a ranked list with position, avatar, name, amount or percent, and a small up/down movement indicator. Engagement mechanics in neo-banks are deliberately limited (streaks, milestones, referral rewards) and the better write-ups insist rewards attach to real financial events, not to opening the app, and that rankings use non-sensitive metrics and avoid shame. → here: Rank by percent of personal target rather than raw sum, so a student with a small target can lead. Show the top of the table plus the viewer's own row with the two neighbours above and below; do not display the bottom. Add 'most improved this week' and a weekly streak counter (weeks in a row with at least one donation). A group-vs-group race bar gives competition without singling anyone out.
- **Empty and first-run states that sell the next step** (Fintech SaaS empty-state galleries and onboarding-checklist case studies (accounting and payments products).): No blank tables. An empty module shows a small illustration or a ghosted preview of what will appear, one sentence of benefit, and a single primary button. New accounts get a setup checklist card with a progress ring ('2 of 5 complete') whose items link straight to the actions; completing an action from an empty state ticks the checklist. Some products seed sample data so the dashboard looks alive on day one. → here: A student with no donations sees their ring at 0% with a ghost chart behind and a card: 'Start your collection' with three steps (add first donor, share your link, record first donation). An empty feed says 'No donations yet today' with the record button. Start of a new year: the hero shows last year's final figure as a faint reference line to beat.

Avoid: A wall of equal-weight KPI tiles and default charts: this is exactly the 'Excel with graphs' look.
